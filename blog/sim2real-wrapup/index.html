World Models for Sim2Real Style Transfer — Project Wrap-up

October 22, 2026 · Jiayu Zhou

0. Thesis and key takeaways

A video world model fine-tuned on 20 real episodes (~10 minutes) from one rig can close the appearance gap between sim and real at usable speed. On a contact-rich insertion task, though, appearance is not the dominant gap: real failures trace back to camera pose, arm settling and placement error, while sim's own failures are contact failures.

  1. Nano is a practical renderer. After 10-step sampling and caching it styles at ~0.06 s per frame in autoregressive episode mode, without chunk flicker. Missing: a standard realism metric and a matched backbone comparison.

  2. Augsim tracks the real rig better than raw sim in aggregate, but not per seed. v4 52k: raw 4/16, augsim 5/16, real 4/16, with augsim sharing 3 of 4 real successes. v5 76k (round-29 cameras): raw sim 11/16, augsim 3/16, real 8/16; per-seed κ vs real = −0.38 (raw) and 0.13 (augsim). For 76k augsim is closer per seed but further off in aggregate, so this claim needs rewording.

  3. Real and sim fail differently. Real: grasp misses traced to a 0.28° camera rotation and a ~3 mm arm-settling bias. Sim: engaged but not seated.

  4. Data beats algorithms for the sim policy. BC on 1024 clean demos (29/32 sim, 8/16 real) beat residual RL, reverse curriculum, distillation and DAgger, each with a diagnosed failure cause.

  5. The sim2real gap is a chain of small errors. It took 29 calibration rounds, a controller port, an SDF contact rebuild, gripper-logic fixes and timing re-measurement; any one of them broke real transfer when wrong.

  6. Scope. RoboRender (2026-10-07) shows the training-data use at scale on simpler tasks; the evaluator role and the contact-rich limit are what this project adds.

2. Claim A: a video world model is a usable sim-to-real renderer

Cosmos3-Nano gives the best trade-off we found between quality, latency and temporal consistency: about 0.06 s per styled frame in autoregressive episode mode after acceleration. The weak point is evidence: no standard image metric (FID, FVD, KID, LPIPS) was ever computed, and the backbones were never compared under one protocol.

The failure modes are disjoint, and that is the useful finding.

  • Real failures are perception or placement failures: 6 grasp misses (seeds …000, 002, 004, 010, 014, 015) and 2 lifted but never engaged (…003, …005).

  • Raw-sim failures are contact failures: all 5 (…005, 007, 008, 009, 012) engage the shaft but never seat.

  • So the real gap sits upstream of contact, while sim's own failures come from contact dynamics. The rig diagnosis confirms it: fingers close 7-11 mm high and aim 5-14 mm toward +y, and an input-swap test traced the +y aim to a 0.28° rotation of the right static camera. Section 6 follows this split.

Still to add:

  • Decide how to reword Claim B given the 76k result: augsim matches the v4 52k aggregate (5 vs 4 real) but undershoots 76k (3 vs 8).

  • Earlier data points: v4 52k with Transfer2.5 and the warp: raw sim 4/16, augsim 5/16, real 4/16 (2026-10-13); augsim shares 3 of the 4 real successes, raw sim only 1 and round-20 step 185600 (raw sim 48/48, augsim 12/16; failed on the real rig on 2026-09-13 because of a controller mismatch, so it is not a clean rendering comparison).

  • Caveat to state up front: n = 16 gives ±20-point intervals. Claim B can only be argued on aggregates and failure modes, not per seed.

4. The road to a near-perfect sim policy: what worked and why the rest failed

Plain behaviour cloning on 1024 clean, full-insertion demos gave the best policy: 29/32 in raw sim and 8/16 on the real rig, the best real result of the project. Every algorithmic add-on either failed or did not survive a rerun. In each case we found a specific cause, not just "RL is hard".

ApproachWhenBest resultDiagnosed reason it stopped
Staged state-based PPO (pick, place, insert)Jun 16-18Chained 94.5% on an eased distribution; 43.75% multi-sideWorked, but state-based; never distilled into a visuomotor policy under realistic gripper physics
SERL / RLPD visuomotor RLJul 18-200% across 3 runsNo learning signal from sparse reward on a contact-rich task
Residual PPO on ACTAug 20-2953.1% → 65.6% (32 seeds); one +15.6-point gain collapsed on rerunExploration noise exceeded the 0.5 mm clearance; base grasp misses (1.4-7.5 cm) were beyond a mm-scale residual
Reverse-curriculum PPO teacherAug 29 - Sep 8Insertion stages 100%; full envelope 78%; grasp stage 68.8%A local optimum (flee basin), unrandomized tooth phase, and five unreachable grasp-gate defects (e.g. 20 mm width gate vs 29 mm physical stall)
Teacher → student distillationSepStudent 37.5%Plateaued, then a dataset bug
DAgger (scripted expert)Sep 5-8Round 1: 18/32 → 29/32Every further round dropped to 4-23/32; the eval env had a physics hook that broke the expert (7/16 vs 16/16)
Residual PPO on the mrc simOct 15-16Training success flat at 69-77%~1.4 s per env step makes RL too slow for the budget; postponed

Two lessons carry beyond this task. Data quality (only full insertions, no recovery behaviour, matched gripper timing) mattered more than any algorithm on top. And in a high-fidelity contact sim, RL costs ~1.4 s per step; that has to be budgeted before RL is chosen.

BC by demo count, for the scaling story: 128 demos → 28/48; 512 → 59-62%; 1024 → 48/48 (round-20 sim) and 29/32 (mrc v5). These come from different sims and protocols, so they are indicative only.

5. Claim C (not yet run): a policy trained in augsim transfers

This is the claim the report still lacks: that a policy trained on styled sim data does well both in augsim and on the real rig. The plan is to style the existing 1024-demo BC dataset rather than run RL in styled space.

Minimum experiment

  1. Style all three cameras of the 1024 demos with Nano (autoregressive mode, same edge + depth conditioning as evaluation).

  2. Train ACT on the styled dataset with the same recipe that produced 76k.

  3. Evaluate on seeds 1000000-1000015 in raw sim, augsim and on the real rig, next to raw-sim-trained 76k.

ComparisonAnswers

Styled-trained, augsim vs realIs augsim a better predictor when the policy has seen styled images?
Styled-trained, raw simHow much does the policy now depend on the style?

Optional extensions that use Nano's architecture

  • Distil the sim teacher into a student that reads Nano's latents instead of decoded pixels, skipping the decode.

  • Style only every k-th frame and reuse the style between keyframes.

  • Use autoregressive styling during distillation for temporal consistency.

Cost: not yet sized. Styling is the expensive part (1024 episodes × frames per episode × 3 cameras at ~16.9 fps at 10 steps). It needs a sizing pass and your approval before anything is submitted.

9. Positioning against RoboRender, and what this approach is good for

RoboRender (Huang, Ai, …, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu; arXiv 2610.09254, 2026-10-07) answers "can a video model make sim data good enough to train real policies" at scale, and the answer is yes for visually driven tasks. Our work answers different questions: how little real data is enough, whether a styled sim can evaluate a policy, and what is left when the task is contact-rich.

Base modelWan2.1-T2V-1.3BCosmos3-Nano 8B
Real training data~127k samples (AgiBot-World-Alpha + DROID), 39,692 scenes~99 episodes (current styler) or 20 demos (retrain), one rig
ConditioningSim depth, robot RGB + mask, structured scene prompt, preceding frameEdge + depth, fixed caption, autoregressive
MultiviewViews stacked into one latent: consistent across camerasEach camera styled independently

TasksPick-and-place, drawer/fridge opening, mobile pickTight-tolerance gear insertion

They also report Cosmos-Transfer2.5 as a baseline at 30% (drawer) and 0% (fridge).

What is still open, and where our evidence speaks

  1. Renderer as evaluator. RoboRender never asks whether a generated sim predicts real outcomes. Our section 3 is direct evidence on that question, including the negative per-seed result.

  2. Contact-rich limit. Our real failures and sim failures are disjoint. A visual-only fix cannot reach the contact-dynamics gap, and their task set never tests it.

  3. Low-data adaptation. About 100 episodes from one rig gave visually convincing output; their recipe needs a large multi-robot corpus.

  4. Gap attribution. A per-stage account of a real2sim2real pipeline (section 6) is not in their paper and is useful whichever renderer wins.

Does video-model style transfer make sense in production? A take to pressure-test:

  • Offline, yes: generating training data and running large-scale evaluation, where seconds per clip are acceptable.

  • In the control loop at 30 Hz, hard to justify, and hallucinated or missing objects (section 8) need a guard.

  • It fixes appearance, not dynamics. For contact-rich tasks it has to be paired with better contact modelling or real interaction data.

  • It should be compared on cost per point of real success against Gaussian-splat real2sim, domain randomization, and simply collecting more real demos.

Sources: RoboRender on arXiv, RoboRender project page.

11. In progress: ref_sim, a sim whose looks are matched to the real frames

The next experiment asks whether a sim tuned to look like the rig, plus domain randomization and image augmentation, makes augsim unnecessary. Each part of the sim image is matched to the real frames; 1024 demos are recollected in this refined sim (ref_sim), a policy is retrained, and it is evaluated in ref_sim, raw sim, augsim and on the real rig.

The visuals are a render-only layer: physics and the control contract are unchanged, so the new policy can be evaluated in the old sim, in augsim and on the rig. Per episode, the collection randomizes lights, colours and textures, specularity, camera exposure and white balance, and the camera pose by about 0.5° and 5 mm (the last real failures came from a 0.28° camera rotation). Training adds LeRobot's image transforms (colour jitter, sharpness, small crop and blur).

Appearance match after nine probe rounds

The render layer took nine probe rounds. The main fixes: a hard arm shadow the rig does not have (round 1); a texture crop that overlapped the real plate and printed a phantom plate on every tile (round 2); grain cut from the real frame that came out flat, then too contrasty, then blotchy after seamless blending (rounds 3–5); the table mirroring the arm as a dark smear (round 6); and whole-table colour calibration against the rig on all three cameras (rounds 4–9). The final grain is synthesized as long streaks along the table axis in the real table's colour. Whole-table median RGB, ref_sim vs rig: wrist2 139/112/67 vs 150/112/72, right static 159/114/68 vs 148/110/62, left static 160/115/68 vs 157/118/67 (raw sim, right static: 75/53/36). The three-camera mean gain is 1.00 / 1.00 / 0.99; the remaining split (statics about 5 % bright, wrist about 7 % dark) is a lighting-direction effect inside the randomization range.

12. Where sim, augsim and the rig part ways: three probes

Augsim and the real rig fail for different reasons. Augsim's failures start at the very first action chunk, where the styled images shift the approach by about 2 cm and the gear ends up in a different place in the hand. The rig differs from sim in dynamics: the real arm tracks the same commands about 3.5 mm differently, enough to break a sub-millimetre insertion.

Probe 1: the real successes' actions, replayed open-loop in sim

For each of the 8 real successes (2026-10-19), the 8-d policy outputs the rig executed were replayed on the same seed in sim. All 8 grasp and lift in sim, but only 2 fully seat. The sim TCP departs from the measured real TCP by more than 5 mm within 2–3 steps and stays 3–4 mm off, with peaks of 6–12 mm during insertion. As a check, feeding the rig's consumed images and state back to the policy reproduces the executed chunks to within 0.006.

Nano is not exactly repeatable run to run. At t = 0 the regenerated frames reproduce the recorded chunk (max abs 0.008–0.015), so those are the true inputs. At later steps the reproduction is off by 0.1–0.8, and up to 1.5–1.9 in the gripper output. Two regenerations of the same t = 0 frame (an earlier, timed-out run) gave plan gaps to raw of 19.5 and 16.5 mm. So Nano's own run-to-run noise moves the plan by about 1–3 mm, the same size as the eval-time vs reconstruction difference. The first-chunk divergence (16–20 mm) is well above that noise.

What this means for ref_sim. Matching appearance can remove the first-chunk shift seen in augsim. It cannot remove the ~3.5 mm tracking gap in probe 1. Modelling the real arm's settling bias is a separate, likely necessary fix.
