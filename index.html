About

I am a master's student in Computer Engineering at Columbia University, where I work on robot learning in Prof. Yunzhu Li's group. I am interested in making simulation and learned world models trustworthy enough to train and evaluate real robots, and in training LLM agents that improve themselves through verifiable feedback. I also work with Prof. Philip S. Yu at the University of Illinois Chicago on agentic reinforcement learning.

Before Columbia, I completed a dual-degree program at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (B.S.) and Zhejiang University (B.E.), where I worked with Prof. Hanghang Tong on LLM reasoning over knowledge graphs and with Prof. Gaoang Wang on personalized image captioning.

Next steps. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027 in robot learning and embodied AI. If my work looks relevant to your group, I would love to hear from you: jz4019@columbia.edu.

Research Interests

  • World models and Sim2Real. Augmenting simulators with learned style-transfer models so that they serve both as faithful evaluators and as scalable data engines for policy learning.
  • Robot learning. Imitation learning, RL post-training, and on-policy distillation for manipulation policies, including VLA models and dexterous grasping.
  • Agentic RL and self-evolving agents. Verifiable rewards for multi-turn tool use, and agents that autonomously build and verify their own skills.

Project Highlights

Augmenting Simulation with World Models for Sim2Real Style Transfer ongoing

Lead author · Prof. Yunzhu Li, Columbia · 2026

Can a simulator dressed up by a style-transfer world model stand in for the real robot, both as an evaluator and as a training-data engine? On a high-precision gear-assembly task with a Franka FR3, policies trained only in augmented simulation reach real-world-level success, and success rates in sim track those on the real robot.

World modelsSim2RealCosmosMuJoCo

Interactive World Model for VLA Imitation Learning and RL Post-Training

Research assistant · Prof. Yunzhu Li, Columbia · 2026

A world model trained on a few hours of real-robot data that rolls out long-horizon, physically consistent trajectories in real time. I used it as a closed-loop data engine for imitation learning and RL post-training of Diffusion Policy, and measured how faithfully it evaluates policies against real-world rollouts.

World modelsDiffusion PolicyRL post-training

DARPA TIAMAT Challenge: Sim-to-Sim Transfer for Boston Dynamics Spot

Perception lead · Columbia team with Prof. Yunzhu Li · 2025–2026

Train a Spot in a low-fidelity simulator, then transfer within a fixed budget to an unseen high-fidelity one. I led perception: domain randomization for training data and a real-time open-set detector that stays accurate on objects it has never seen, served to the navigation and locomotion stacks.

FuncGrasp: Semantically-Guided Functional Dexterous Grasping

Course project · Prof. Yunzhu Li, Columbia · 2025

Grasping an object the way a task needs it, not just stably. A vision-language model turns instructions and multi-view observations into contact maps, and a diffusion policy turns those into Shadow Hand actions.

CoEvoSkills: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Co-Evolutionary Verification COLM 2026

With Prof. Philip S. Yu, UIC · 2026

Agents that write and verify their own multi-file skills. A skill generator and an isolated verifier co-evolve without ever seeing the ground-truth tests, and the resulting skills beat human-written ones on SkillsBench and transfer across model families.

ToolTurn: Multi-Turn Tool-Use Agent RL with Graph-Guided Data Synthesis IEEE BigData 2026

With Prof. Philip S. Yu, UIC · 2025–2026

A tool-dependency graph that both synthesizes multi-turn tool-use trajectories and serves as a sandbox with verifiable per-turn rewards, so that RL for tool-using agents no longer depends on a sparse end-of-episode signal.

EmbRAG: LLM Reasoning over Incomplete Temporal Knowledge Graphs

Research assistant · Prof. Hanghang Tong, UIUC · 2023–2025

Letting an LLM reason over knowledge graphs that are missing edges: sampled and scored rules guide the model, and embedding-based gap filling makes the whole pipeline end-to-end trainable, reaching state-of-the-art results on temporal knowledge-graph benchmarks.
